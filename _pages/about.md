## A little about me

I'm just a **happy person**. That's honestly the most accurate thing I can tell you about myself.

I look for the green and colourful parts of life, even when things get messy. And they do get messy. I fall. I fail. But I have this thing where I refuse to stay down, not because I'm stubborn, but because I genuinely believe things work out if you keep going.

It's a little like our guy Santiago from Hemingway's *The Old Man and the Sea*. I'm aspirational like that. I have this glorious delusion that things will work out eventually. Big dreams, for myself and, honestly, for the world too.

Professionally, I'm interested in the intersection of **business, people, operations and data**. I enjoy looking at a problem, figuring out what's really happening beneath the surface, and finding a way to make things work better.

Here's a little more about the person behind the page.

### The professional journey

I have a bachelor's degree in Mechanical Engineering from Jawaharlal Nehru Technological University, but life had other plans.

I started my career as a Procurement Specialist at a family-run architectural firm in Hyderabad. I managed procurement portfolios worth over $1 million, analysed procurement spend, designed and implemented supplier performance processes, streamlined vendor onboarding, and conducted inventory demand analysis.

I worked with Excel (PivotTables and VLOOKUP), Power BI, Tableau and SAP Analytics. I came in with foundational knowledge and developed my capabilities through online courses, hands-on practice and daily use. I'm a firm believer that you can learn a great deal by getting your hands dirty and figuring things out.

After the company shut down operations, I had a brief stint as a Verification Associate at Absolute People Screen Pvt. Ltd. There, I put my Excel and SQL skills to work and brought an analytical approach to background verification operations. I built a real-time Tableau dashboard to monitor verification activity and developed structured workflows using SQL and Excel, replacing ad hoc approaches with a repeatable, data-driven process. The improvements helped increase case accuracy by 25% and reduce turnaround time by 30%.

Then came customer service at [24]7.ai and Amazon. At the time, it felt like a detour. It turned out to be one of the best educations I've ever had.

I spent years talking to people on some of their worst days: angry, frustrated and let down. What I learned is that every business in the world is really just a people problem. If you don't have customers, you've no business. That's it. It's as simple as that.

Over nearly five years, I grew through three levels, from frontline execution to strategic analysis and team leadership. I progressed from Digital Interaction Associate to Executive and then Specialist. I led a team of 10 customer service representatives, setting performance KPIs, translating service metrics into actionable coaching and managing team progress towards departmental targets.

At one point, our team was drowning in DSAT scores — customer dissatisfaction — and nobody quite knew why. I was handed a massive data file and told to figure it out. And I did. I found the patterns, traced them back to their sources, coached the team and watched things change.

At Amazon, I worked as an operational analyst and cross-continental process leader. I conducted weekly training sessions for support teams across the US and India, communicating process updates, system changes and escalation protocols in a structured, consistent way. The work helped reduce knowledge gaps and improve first-contact resolution.

Looking back at all those experiences, I had a moment when I thought: **this is what I want to do.**

So I moved to Toronto, completed a business management course, and here I am — learning every day, building skills and looking for work where I can solve problems that matter.

### Outside of work

When I'm not buried in work, I'm usually reading - mostly fiction.

I used to be a heavy non-fiction reader until it dawned on me that fiction is actually where the real truth lives. Between the lines, in the things characters don't say, and in the little details that reveal more than entire chapters of explanation.

As Ben Okri writes in *A Way of Being Free*:

> Beware the stories you read or tell; subtly, at night, beneath the waters of consciousness, they are altering your world.

I write too, mostly essays, fiction and journal entries. If I feel confident enough, I might post some of my writing here someday.

Writing, for me, is just thinking out loud with better posture.

I'm also interested in fitness and bodybuilding, chess, learning guitar, languages, data and the many ways technology can help us understand the world. I like learning things that make me think differently.

And I suppose that's the thread connecting most of my interests: curiosity. I like understanding how things work, why people behave the way they do, and what might happen if we look at a problem from another angle.

### A final thought

If you value curiosity, warmth, empathy, and you're compassionate and someone who genuinely gives a damn, you should reach out.

I think we'd get along!
